RGB Screen Simulator

How does a phone, computer or TV screen show millions of colours? Find out in three short steps. It takes about two minutes, and you do not need to know anything about computers.

This box looks yellow. But your screen has no yellow light inside it. So where does the yellow come from?

A screen is made of tiny dots

Every picture on a screen is built from thousands of tiny dots called pixels. They are so small that you normally cannot see them. Here is a picture. Let’s look closer and closer.

Each dot has only three lights: red, green and blue

Did you see it? The sun had no yellow in it, only red and green stripes. Every pixel has just three small lights: one red, one green and one blue. They never change colour. They can only get brighter or dimmer.

3

The lights never mix. Your eyes do the mixing.

On the screen, the three lights always stay side by side. But when they are small enough, your eye cannot tell them apart, so it blends them into one colour. Making colours by adding lights together like this is called additive colour mixing.

What you learned

  1. A screen is made of tiny dots called pixels.
  2. Each pixel has only three lights: red, green and blue. They only change brightness.
  3. The lights never mix on the screen. They are too small to see one by one, so your eyes blend them into one colour.

Words to know

Pixel
One dot of the screen.
RGB
Red, green and blue: the three lights in every pixel.
Additive colour mixing
Making colours by adding lights together.
LCD and OLED
The two main kinds of screen. They make light in different ways, but both use red, green and blue lights.

On page 1 you saw that red and green light together look yellow. This page shows why.

1

Your eye has only three colour sensors

Your eye has three kinds of colour sensors (called cones): one for red light, one for green and one for blue. Your brain cannot see the light itself. It only knows how strongly each sensor reacts. If two lights make the sensors react the same way, they look like the same colour.

How the three colour sensors in your eye react

Each bar is one sensor inside your eye. A longer bar means that sensor reacts more strongly. Your brain reads the three bars together and turns them into one colour.

These bars are not controls. They only show the result. To change the colour, use the three sliders above.

Your brain only gets three numbers: how strongly each sensor reacts. It cannot tell whether those numbers came from one pure light or from a mix of red, green and blue. So with just three lights, a screen can make your brain see almost any colour.

For example, the yellow of a real lemon is its own kind of light. A screen has no such light. It sends only red and green light, which is something else, but your brain creates the very same yellow from that mix.
